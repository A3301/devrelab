Background · Same Mathematics, the Only Difference Is the Formula for α

The series RLC is the “dual” of the parallel circuit (duality): voltage ↔ current, KCL ↔ KVL, R ↔ 1/R.

1 · Intuition: who dissipates the energy?

In the series circuit the current has a single path and always flows through R: as R increases more energy turns into heat and damping increases. In the parallel circuit, R is a “leakage path” for the L–C oscillation: as R increases the leakage decreases and damping decreases. Easy check: set R → 0; the series circuit becomes a lossless LC (α → 0), while in the parallel circuit R is a short circuit (α → ∞).

Series RLC is everywhere: the inductance of a long cable with the input capacitance of a receiver (ringing on digital lines), parasitic LC in switch-mode power supplies, the ignition coil in vehicles, the wave-shaping circuit of defibrillators, the series-resonant filter of a radio receiver.

⚠ Points to watch in a real lab

An ignition coil is dangerous even if not lethal. The secondary produces 15–40 kV; a shock can cause falls through muscle contraction and serious consequences for people with pacemakers. Do not touch a running coil circuit, do not hold the spark-plug lead by hand, use insulated pliers and a suitable HV probe.
Interrupting inductor current = high voltage. When the switch opens in a series circuit, v = L di/dt appears across the contacts. Put a freewheeling diode or an RC snubber on every switch that interrupts a relay or motor coil.
Discharge the capacitor. In a series RLC, vC may exceed the source voltage after a step (Example 4.6: 67 V with a 48 V source). The capacitor’s voltage rating must be chosen according to the peak value, not the source; discharge it through a resistor at the end of the experiment.
Oscilloscope probe and ground spring. When measuring nanosecond ringing, a long ground clip itself adds 100–200 nH of inductance and shows false ringing. Use the probe’s short ground spring and measure in the 10× position.
Electrolytic capacitors and reverse voltage. In an underdamped series circuit vC may change sign (natural response). Do not use a polarised capacitor; choose a film capacitor.
Resistor power rating and inductance. The damping resistor must not be wire-wound (its own inductance increases ringing); choose carbon film or metal film. In the battery circuit the ballast resistor produces several watts of heat.
Common mistakes: using α = 1/2RC in the series circuit (check with R → 0!) · mixing up the critical conditions (½√(L/C) ↔ 2√(L/C)) · not including the generator’s internal resistance in R in the series circuit · forgetting that vC(∞) = Vs and i(∞) = 0 in the series step · assuming the overshoot cannot exceed the source.

    Experiment 2 · Ringing with a Square Wave and the Damping Resistor

    A logic gate drives a 1 nF load through a cable with 1 µH inductance: this is a series RLC. CH1 shows the driver output, CH2 the voltage at the load; the rising edge is at the 1st division.

    Lab sheet

      Notes

      The series R is the driver’s internal resistance + the line resistance + the added Rd: α = (Rg + r + Rd)/(2L) The line’s “characteristic resistance” √(L/C) is 31.6 Ω here.
      The ringing frequency is almost ω0; the time between two successive peaks is Td = 2π/ωd.
      A real cable is a distributed LC line; here it is modelled as lumped with a single L and C. This model is good if the edge time is much longer than the line delay.
      The ringing of a 5 V logic signal can shoot up to 8–9 V: the receiver’s input protection diodes conduct and the IC may be damaged. To see the ringing on the oscilloscope use the probe’s short ground spring.

      Experiment 3 · Ignition Coil: High-Voltage Generation with LC

      While the contact breaker is closed, the battery drives current through the coil primary (RL charging). At t = 0 the breaker opens: the primary inductance and the capacitor across the breaker form a series RLC; the primary voltage rises to hundreds of volts and the secondary (×100) to tens of thousands of volts.

      Lab sheet

        Notes

        During the closed (dwell) time: ip(t) = (Vb/R)(1 − e−t/τ), τ = Lp/R
        When the breaker opens, energy passes from the coil to the capacitor: ½LpI0² ≈ ½Cvmax² → vmax ≈ I0√(Lp/C) Secondary voltage ≈ N·vp.
        The same principle is used in the boost converter, camera flash and electric fence drivers: energy stored in the inductor appears as high voltage when the current is interrupted.
        Safety: In a real ignition circuit the secondary is 15–40 kV. While it is running do not touch the coil, the spark-plug lead or the distributor; measure only with a suitable HV probe; people with pacemakers must not take part in this experiment. Electronic ignition modules also produce 300–400 V on the primary.
