Background · Combining Elements and Sharing Flux

The series–parallel rules are sometimes the same as for resistors and sometimes the opposite. In mutual inductance, the sign is everything.

1 · Series and parallel: what is shared?

Parallel elements share the voltage; series elements share the current (for capacitors, the charge). Inductors behave like resistors: they add in series, and their reciprocals add in parallel. For capacitors the rule is reversed: they add in parallel (plate area grows), and their reciprocals add in series (the dielectric gets thicker). In series capacitors the voltage is not shared equally: the smaller capacitance takes the larger voltage.

2 · Mutual inductance

Part of the flux of one winding also passes through the other winding. When this shared flux changes, a voltage is induced in the second winding: v2 = M di1/dt  (i2 = 0) A constant current induces nothing: a transformer does not work on DC. The coupling coefficient k tells how much of the flux is shared: M = k√(L₁L₂), 0 ≤ k ≤ 1. Transformers, wireless charging, current transformers and isolated power supplies work on this principle; unwanted coupling, on the other hand, produces crosstalk on PCBs.

⚠ Points to watch in a real laboratory

Do not interrupt an inductor current suddenly. When the circuit of a current-carrying inductor is opened, L·di/dt becomes very large: a spark (back EMF) appears at the switch and hundreds of volts may occur. This pulse also appears in the coupled second winding in the ratio M/L₁; do not touch the open secondary, and if necessary place a freewheeling diode or an RC snubber across the winding.
Do not connect a charged capacitor to the LCR meter. After the pre-charged capacitor experiment, discharge the capacitors through a resistor and measure that they are at 0 V; otherwise the input stage of the LCR meter will be damaged. The LCR meter measures with an AC signal anyway; it cannot see the initial voltage.
Voltage rating of series capacitors. If 2 µF/16 V and 4 µF/16 V are connected in series and 30 V is applied, the 2 µF one sees 20 V and breaks down. Do not check the total “32 V”; check each capacitor’s own share.
A real inductor has resistance. The LCR meter measures the inductor as a series L–R; the winding resistance (Rs) and the quality factor Q are also read. When driving with a current source, watch for inductor heating and the wire current limit; R increases as the winding heats up.
Coupling also happens unintentionally. If you place two inductors that you will measure separately next to each other, an M appears between them and your series/parallel measurement comes out wrong. For an “uncoupled” measurement, place the inductors far apart with perpendicular axes. An iron table leg, a screwdriver or a magnet next to the inductor also changes L.
Oscilloscope grounds are common. When monitoring the primary (current shunt) and the secondary winding at the same time, the grounds of the two probes are joined inside the oscilloscope. Connecting one end of the secondary winding to circuit ground removes the insulation between the windings; never do this on a high-voltage transformer, use a differential probe.
Common mistakes: adding series capacitors like resistors (8 µF instead of 1.5 µF for 2+6); confusing the sign of M with the sign of the derivative (the dots determine ±M, the derivative determines the sign of the number); writing M = (L₊−L₋)/2 (the correct one is /4); comparing M with a single inductor (the limit is √(L₁L₂)); assuming different initial currents in series inductors; forgetting to add the initial currents of parallel inductors.

    Experiment 2 · Mutual Inductance: Dot Convention, M and k

    The primary winding (L₁ = 40 mH) is driven by a V→I current source; CH1 shows i₁ across a 1 Ω shunt (1 V = 1 A), CH2 shows the open-circuit secondary voltage (L₂ = 90 mH, dotted terminal “+”). Adjust the coupling by changing the spacing between the windings.

    Lab sheet

      Information

      With the secondary open (the oscilloscope’s 1 MΩ draws no current) only the mutual term remains: v₂ = M di₁/dt With a triangular current the slope is constant (±2Ippf), so v₂ is a square wave: M = V₂,peak / (2Ippf)
      If i₁ enters the dotted terminal and is increasing, the dotted terminal of the secondary is positive; if it is decreasing, it is negative. The dots and the derivative together determine the sign.
      As the spacing increases the shared flux decreases, and k and M become smaller. You cannot read k directly; measure M and calculate k = M/√(L₁L₂).
      When driving an inductor with a current source, if the source output becomes “open circuit” (a lead comes off) the source voltage hits its ceiling; change connections with the power off. Asking for a square current means very large L·di/dt voltages at the edges.

      Experiment 3 · Series Aiding/Opposing Connection and Finding the Dot Polarity Experimentally

      A coil pair with erased labels: the dot of the first winding (terminal 1) is known, that of the second winding is not. Connect the windings in series and measure them with the LCR meter in two different ways.

      Lab sheet

        Information

        In a series connection, if the current enters the dotted terminal of both windings, the fluxes add: L₊ = L₁ + L₂ + 2M If it enters one and leaves the other, the fluxes weaken each other: L₋ = L₁ + L₂ − 2M The connection with the larger reading is the aiding connection; the dot position follows from this.
        The difference of the two measurements is 4M: M = (L₊ − L₋)/4 If L₁ and L₂ are also measured, k = M/√(L₁L₂) is found.
        Workshop method (for information): a battery and a switch are connected to the primary, an analog (centre-zero) voltmeter to the secondary. If the voltmeter needle deflects to the right when the switch closes, the terminal connected to the battery’s + and the terminal connected to the voltmeter’s + are like-dotted terminals.
        In the battery–switch method, a large back EMF appears in the primary when the switch is opened and the needle kicks hard in the opposite direction; a meter on a sensitive range may be damaged. Start on a high range.

        Experiment 4 · Energy in Coupled Inductors and the k ≤ 1 Limit

        L₁ = 40 mH, L₂ = 90 mH. Adjustable DC currents are applied to the two windings (positive direction: into the dotted terminal). While the currents are built up from zero one after the other, the total energy delivered by the sources is measured with an energy meter (∫v·i dt).

        Lab sheet

          Information

          Building the currents one after the other: first i₁ (only ½L₁I₁²), then i₂ is built up while I₁ is constant; meanwhile the primary source does work M·I₁·I₂ against the mutual voltage: w = ½L₁i₁² + ½L₂i₂² + M i₁i₂ If i₂ leaves the dot (i₂ < 0) the mutual term reduces the energy.
          As a function of the ratio i₂/i₁, the energy takes its minimum value at i₂ = −(M/L₂) i₁: wmin = ½L₁i₁²(1 − k²) A passive network cannot store negative energy; therefore k ≤ 1 is mandatory.
          In reality, applying DC to two windings and suddenly opening one causes the stored energy to discharge in a spark. Switch off by slowly reducing the currents to zero at the sources.
