Exam Preparation Summary · Chapters 1 and 2 on One Page

Final review before the midterm: the main idea of both chapters, the rotating-phasor intuition, all formulas on one card and the 10 most common mistakes.

1 · The essence of Chapter 1: jω instead of the derivative

In a linear circuit all responses to a sinusoidal source have the same frequency; only amplitude and phase change. So we can think of every sinusoid as the shadow of a rotating arrow (phasor): V = Vm∠θ. Differentiation becomes multiplication by jω; the differential equation reduces to algebra. R, L, C each become an impedance (R, jωL, −j/ωC), and every tool we know from DC (series/parallel, voltage divider, Thevenin, node/mesh) works just the same, but with complex numbers.

Rotating phasor ↔ sinusoid

The arrow rotates at speed ω; its horizontal projection (Re) is v(t). The blue arrow is the current: lagging by θ (inductive load). Because both arrows rotate at the same speed, the angle between them never changes: a phasor diagram is a photograph of this frozen instant.

2 · The essence of Chapter 2: P does work, Q goes back and forth

p(t) = P + P cos2ωt − Q sin2ωt. Its average P (W) turns into heat/work in the load; Q (VAR) goes back and forth between the field and the source. Everything is collected in one complex number: S = VrmsIrms* = P + jQ, |S| is the apparent power (VA), pf = P/|S|. A capacitor next to a motor supplies its Q (pf correction); for the load to draw maximum power, ZL = ZTh*.

The 10 most common mistakes

  1. Taking the phase without converting sine to cosine (sin(ωt − 15°) → −105°).
  2. Adding magnitudes: |VR| + |VC| ≠ |V|; phasors add as vectors.
  3. Mixing degrees and radians: 1000πt is in radians, the phase angle in degrees; check your calculator mode.
  4. Forgetting the minus sign of capacitor impedance: ZC = −j/(ωC).
  5. Mixing f and ω: XL = 2πfL; if “ω = 1000” then f = 159.15 Hz.
  6. Mixing peak and rms: P = ½VmImcos θ but P = VrmsIrmscos θ; do not use the ½ factor twice.
  7. Using the power factor without its sign: “lagging” is inductive (Q > 0), “leading” is capacitive (Q < 0).
  8. Writing S = VI: the correct form is S = V·I* (conjugate of the current); otherwise the sign of Q comes out reversed.
  9. Taking ZL = ZTh for maximum power: the correct condition is the conjugate, ZTh*.
  10. Using the transformer ratio upside down: the reflected impedance is (N1/N2)²ZL, and the current ratio is the inverse of the voltage ratio.
Solved mini example (Examples 1.16 + 2.3 together): vs = 50 cos1000t V, R = 30 Ω, L = 60 mH, C = 50 µF in series. Z = 30 + j(60 − 20) = 50∠53.13° Ω → I = 1∠−53.13° A. P = ½·50·1·cos53.13° = 15 W = ½Im²R ✓; Q = 20 VAR; VL = 60 V > source 50 V (normal for phasors!).

⚠ Points to watch in a real lab

When troubleshooting, measure the source first. The function generator has a 50 Ω internal resistance: with a load connected, the terminal voltage is lower than the set value. Measure first at the generator terminals, then across each element; compare the V/I ratios with the current shown by the ammeter.
Multimeter AC range and frequency. Cheap multimeters read rms correctly only at 50–400 Hz; at kHz they read low. With square/triangle waves, a meter that is not “true rms” gives a wrong reading.
Never measure resistance/capacitance in a live circuit. The ohm and capacitance ranges apply their own signal; in an energised circuit they read wrong and stress the meter. To measure a component value, switch the power off and disconnect one of its leads (LCR meter).
At resonance the element voltage exceeds the source. In a series RLC, VC = Q·V is possible: seeing 100 V on the capacitor with a 10 V source is possible. Choose the capacitor voltage rating accordingly.
Oscilloscope ground. The ground clips of both probes must go to the same node. For mains-related measurements use an isolation transformer and a differential probe; clipping the probe ground to the line conductor creates a short circuit and an arc.
Never leave a current transformer secondary open; never short a voltage transformer secondary. If the secondary of a current transformer is opened, a very high voltage appears. Shorting the secondary of a voltage transformer burns the winding.
Charged capacitor and inductor current. After switching off, discharge the capacitor through a resistor; do not interrupt inductor current suddenly with a switch (L di/dt spark).

Task 1 · Find the Fault: Series RLC Board

The board should be as in the schematic: R = 220 Ω, L = 22 mH (winding resistance 8 Ω), C = 1 µF; generator 5 V rms, internal resistance 50 Ω. Each new board may have a random fault (or no fault at all). Place the red and black leads on the test points (TP), measure, and choose your diagnosis.

Lab sheet

    Information

    On a healthy board at 1 kHz: Z = 278 − j20.9 Ω (including generator) I ≈ 18 mA, VR ≈ 3.9 V, VL ≈ 2.5 V, VC ≈ 2.9 V. The sum of the element voltages (≈ 9.3 V) is larger than the source: normal for phasors.
    Why do we measure like this? In a series circuit the current is the same everywhere. If the current is zero, something is open, and the full source voltage appears across that opening. If an element has zero voltage but current flows, that element is shorted. If current flows but the voltages are out of proportion, a value is wrong: calculate |Zk| = Vk/I and compare with the expected value.
    Changing the frequency separates L from C: as f increases, XL grows and XC shrinks. How does the voltage of the suspect element change with f?
    On a real board a punctured (shorted) capacitor may heat up; set faulty components aside as e-waste, do not throw them in the bin. Switch the power off while looking for a solder bridge.

    Task 2 · Design: Setting the Phase Angle with a Series Element

    A power amplifier feeds an inductive load (Ry + Ly, e.g. a model of a relay/solenoid coil) at 1 kHz. Add a series capacitor or inductor to the load so that the current reaches the target phase angle relative to the voltage. Also choose the element’s voltage/current rating.

    Lab sheet

      Information

      Target: θ = θv − θi = arctan((Xy + Xs)/Ry) Required series reactance Xs = Ry tan θtarget − Xy If Xs < 0, a capacitor (C = 1/(ω|Xs|)); if Xs > 0, an inductor (L = Xs/ω).
      E12 values come in coarse steps; you may combine two elements: capacitors in parallel (C1 + C2), inductors in series (L1 + L2). Acceptance: |θ − θtarget| ≤ 3°.
      Rating choice: the capacitor’s peak voltage √2·VC must not exceed its rated voltage; the inductor’s rms current must not exceed its rated current. A higher rating than needed is expensive: choose the cheapest sufficient rating.
      Why does it matter? The same idea underlies power factor correction, resonant circuits and phase shifters. A series capacitor “cancels” inductive reactance; but as you approach resonance the current and element voltage grow.
      The voltage on a series capacitor may be larger than the source voltage. A capacitor with a wrong (too low) voltage rating will be punctured; polarized electrolytics are not used with AC.

      Task 3 · Identifying a Load from an Oscilloscope Screen or a Phasor Diagram

      There is a load in the black box. The screen shows either an oscilloscope display (CH1 v, CH2 i, current probe 1 V/A) or a scaled phasor diagram. Find the load type, the angle θv − θi and the average power.

      Lab sheet

        Information

        On the oscilloscope: which one peaks first? If v peaks first, the current lags → inductive (θ > 0). Angle: θ = 360° · Δt / T Power: P = ½VmIm cos θ
        On a phasor diagram angles are measured from the horizontal axis, counter-clockwise positive: θ = ∠V − ∠I Lengths in the diagram are peak values.
        Why identification? In the field you often cannot see inside the load; you only see v and i at its terminals. The phase relation tells you the character of the load, the amplitude ratio gives |Z|, and together they give P and Q.
        In a real measurement, if you take CH2 across a shunt, clip the ground lead to the ground side of the shunt; fit the current probe with its arrow in the direction of the current, otherwise i appears inverted by 180°.
