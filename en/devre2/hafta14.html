Background · How Does a Wattmeter Work, and Why Are Two Wattmeters Enough?

Understanding what is inside an instrument is the best way to avoid connecting it wrongly.

1 · Electrodynamic wattmeter

The magnetic field of the fixed (current) coil is proportional to i, and the current of the moving (voltage) coil is proportional to v. The torque turning the pointer is proportional to their product, i.e. to p(t) = v·i; the inertia of the pointer shows its average: W = V I cos θ. The “±” terminals must be connected to the same point (the source side); if one is reversed, the pointer deflects backwards.

Why are two wattmeters enough? (Blondel) In a 3-wire system Ia + Ib + Ic = 0. If we measure the voltages with respect to line b, p = vabia + vcbic; the term for b vanishes. For an n-wire system n − 1 wattmeters are enough, even if the load is unbalanced.

In the acb sequence W₁ and W₂ swap places. Some sources use the names W₁ and W₂ the other way round (tan θ = √3(W₁ − W₂)/(W₁ + W₂)); always state which wattmeter is on which line.

Worked mini example (Example 3.28): abc, VL = 400 V, W₁ = 2000 W, W₂ = 4000 W. ① P = 6000 W. ② Q = √3·2000 = 3464 VAR. ③ tan θ = 0.577 ⇒ θ = 30°, pf = 0.866 lagging (W₂ > W₁ ⇒ inductive). ④ |S| = 6928 VA ⇒ IL = 6928/(√3·400) = 10 A ⇒ Zφ = 230.9/10∠30° = 20 + j11.55 Ω.

2 · How do W₁ and W₂ change with the load angle?

At θ = 0 the two wattmeters read the same. With an inductive load W₂ grows and W₁ shrinks; at θ = 60° (pf = 0.5) W₁ is zero, and beyond that it is negative. With a capacitive load the opposite happens. From the ratio of the readings you can estimate the pf at a glance.

⚠ Points to watch in a real laboratory

Never connect the current coil in parallel with the source. The current coil has almost zero resistance: connecting it in parallel is a short circuit and the coil burns instantly. Make connections de-energized and check them one by one against the schematic.
Wattmeter overload cannot be seen from the pointer. With a low power factor load, the current or voltage coil range may be exceeded even while the pointer is mid-scale. Always add an ammeter and a voltmeter to the circuit; select the highest ranges first, then reduce.
Reverse-deflecting pointer. If the pointer hits the stop left of zero, the coil spring is strained. Switch off immediately, swap the terminals of the voltage (or current) coil and note the reading with a minus sign; some wattmeters have a polarity switch for this.
Measurement with current transformers. For large currents the wattmeter is connected through a current transformer; its secondary is never opened while the circuit is live (high voltage, insulation breakdown). A voltage transformer secondary, on the other hand, is never short-circuited.
Capacitor bank and black box. A load whose contents you do not know may contain capacitors; after disconnecting, do not touch its terminals without measuring them. In a 400 V system the peak voltage is 565 V.
Common mistakes: adding a negative reading as its absolute value · using the cos(θ ± 30°) formula for an unbalanced load · taking θ for the angle between line voltage and line current · trusting the sign of tan θ without knowing the phase sequence · applying Q = √3·W to an unbalanced load · connecting the wattmeter’s “±” terminals to different sides.

    Info

    The full scale of an analog wattmeter is Wfull = Vrange × Irange (for pf = 1). 300 V × 5 A = 1500 W.
    Why does the pointer deflect backwards? The torque is proportional to the average of v·i. When the voltage coil terminals are reversed, the sign of v changes and the average becomes negative. A real reversal of power flow (e.g. a motor running as a generator) gives the same result.
    Mini example: 40 + j30 Ω ⇒ |Z| = 50 Ω, I = 4.6 A, cos θ = 0.8 ⇒ W = 230·4.6·0.8. Calculate before measuring and compare with the pointer.
    Range selection: first check the current with an ammeter. For example, for 4.6 A the 5 A range is suitable; the 1 A range burns the coil, the 10 A range halves the reading resolution.

    3-wire three-phase system, balanced Y or Δ load. W₁ and W₂ are analog; for a reverse-deflecting wattmeter reverse its voltage coil and record the reading as negative (a minus is entered in the table automatically).

    Lab sheet

      Info

      For a balanced abc load: W₁ = VLILcos(θ + 30°), W₂ = VLILcos(θ − 30°) θ is the angle of the phase impedance (in both Y and Δ).
      Why ±30°? W₁ sees the line voltage Vab, which leads Van by 30°. Ia lags Van by θ: the angle between them is θ + 30°. For W₂, Vcb lags Vcn by 30°: the angle is θ − 30°.

      For lightly loaded motors with pf < 0.5 one wattmeter always deflects backwards; this is not a fault. If you forget to add the reading with a minus sign, the power comes out several times too large.

          Info
