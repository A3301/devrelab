Background · The Delta Connection and the Secret of Three-Phase Power

How are currents shared in a Δ load, why does power flow at a constant rate, and why does line loss depend on the power factor?

1 · Δ (delta) connection

In Δ each load branch is connected between two lines: branch voltage = line voltage. But each line feeds two branches at once; KCL at node A: IaA = IAB − ICA. The difference of two equal currents 120° apart is √3 times larger and (in abc) lags by 30°. There is no neutral: a Δ load is fed by 3 wires.

Why the Δ–Y transformation? If we replace a balanced Δ load with a Y load whose branches are ZY = ZΔ/3, every measurement seen from the terminals stays the same; so we can use the single-phase equivalent circuit of Week 12 unchanged.

Formula card

Voltage in ΔVφ = VL
Current in Δ (abc)IaA = √3 IAB∠−30° (acb: ∠+30°)
Δ → Y (balanced)ZY = ZΔ/3
Total active powerPT = 3VφIφcos θ = √3 VLILcos θ
Reactive / apparentQT = √3VLILsin θ, |ST| = √3VLIL
Complex powerST = 3 Vφ Iφ* = PT + jQT
Total instantaneous powerpa+pb+pc = PT (constant)
Line lossPline = 3IL²Rℓ, IL = PT/(√3VLpf)
pf correctionQC = P(tan θ1 − tan θ2), CΔ = QC/(3ωVL²)

θ is always the angle of the phase impedance (not the angle between line voltage and line current).

2 · Instantaneous power: one phase pulsates, three phases flow

In a single phase p(t) = VI cos θ + VI cos(2ωt − θ): power pulsates at 100 Hz and, if θ ≠ 0, briefly becomes negative (energy returns to the source). In three phases the three pulsating terms are 120°·2 = 240° apart and their sum is zero: pT = 3VφIφcos θ is constant. Vibration-free operation of large motors is the real advantage of three-phase systems.

Worked mini example (Example 3.18): Z = 30 + j40 Ω, 400 V. Y: Vφ = 230.9 V, I = 4.62 A, P = 3·4.62²·30 = 1920 W. Δ: Vφ = 400 V, Iφ = 8 A, IL = 13.86 A, P = 3·8²·30 = 5760 W. Ratio: 3. If the same motor is started in Y and run in Δ, the starting current drops to one third.

⚠ Points to watch in a real laboratory

Links in the motor terminal box. Change the Y/Δ links only while the supply is disconnected and locked out. Connecting a motor rated 230/400 V in Δ on a 400 V supply applies √3 times the voltage to the windings and burns the motor.
Capacitor banks remain charged after disconnection from the supply. Check that the discharge resistors work; before touching, wait at least 1–5 minutes and short the terminals with an earthed rod. The capacitor peak voltage is √2·VL = 849 V (600 V system).
Never leave a current-transformer secondary open. Power analyzers measure large currents through current transformers; when removing the meter the secondary must be shorted first, otherwise thousands of volts are induced.
Instrument limits. Power analyzer voltage inputs are usually limited to 600 V–1000 V CAT III. If the direction of the current clamps (arrow from source to load) is wrong, power appears negative.
Overcompensation and resonance. Too much capacitance raises the voltage and, on a grid with harmonics, can cause resonance and blow fuses. In practice, stepped banks controlled by a reactive power relay and, if needed, harmonic filters are used.
Common mistakes: mixing up line/phase quantities in Y and Δ · using θ ± 30° instead of θ · taking IL in Δ for the branch current · thinking “the voltages sum to zero, so power is zero too” · thinking P also changes with pf correction · using the Y formula for a Δ bank (factor of 3 difference).

    Why does one line current stay the same when a branch opens? The current in the open CA branch is zero; lines A and C now feed only one branch each (IaA = IAB), while line B still feeds two. A Δ load keeps working with one branch open, but becomes unbalanced.

    Experiment 2 · Δ–Y Transformation: Same Windings, Y or Δ?

    Three identical windings are connected in Y or Δ in the terminal box. With the supply connected, current and power are measured; with the supply disconnected, the impedance seen from the terminals is measured with an LCR bridge.

    Lab sheet

      Info

      In Y a winding sees VL/√3, IL = Iφ. In Δ a winding sees VL, IL = √3 Iφ. Result: IL,Δ = 3 IL,Y, PΔ = 3 PY
      Why ZY = ZΔ/3? Looking between two terminals, in Y two branches are in series (2ZY); in Δ one branch is in parallel with the series of the other two: Z ∥ 2Z = 2Z/3. The two must be equal: 2ZY = 2ZΔ/3.
      An LCR bridge is used only on a de-energized circuit; an LCR bridge connected to a live winding is destroyed instantly. On large motor windings make sure any residual voltage has decayed before measuring.

      Experiment 3 · Three-Phase Power and Constant Total Instantaneous Power

      Circuit of Example 3.19: generator (Zg), line (Zℓ), Y load (4-wire). The power analyzer can be connected at three points; the oscilloscope shows p = v·i with its MATH function.

      Lab sheet

        Info

        Power balance: Semf = Sint + Sline + Sload The active share of each part is 3|I|²R, the reactive share 3|I|²X.
        Why is pT constant? pk = V I [cos θ + cos(2ωt − θ − 2·120°k)]. The phases of the three cosines are 0°, −240°, −480° (≡ −120°): like three balanced phasors, their sum is zero. Imbalance breaks this symmetry.
        Solution path (Example 3.19): I = 2.4 A (Week 12). Pload = 3·5.76·39 = 673.9 W; Qload = 3·5.76·28 = 483.8 VAR; Pline = 3·5.76·0.8 = 13.8 W; Semf = 3·120·2.4∠36.87°.
        The arrow direction of the power analyzer’s current clamps must point from source to load, and the phase sequence must match the voltage leads (clamp a on line a). Otherwise P comes out negative or meaningless.

        Experiment 4 · Line Loss and Three-Phase Power Factor Correction

        A 480 kW factory load with pf = 0.8 lagging; line 0.005 + j0.025 Ω/phase; VL = 600 V at the load bus (Examples 3.21–3.24). A Δ-connected capacitor bank is added at the load bus.

        Lab sheet

          Info

          A capacitor draws no active power; it only supplies the inductive reactive current of the load locally. The line now carries only P and the remaining Q: IL = P/(√3 VL pf) As pf rises, current, loss and voltage drop decrease.
          Why connect in Δ? In Δ each capacitor sees VL; since Q ∝ V², one third of the capacitance of a Y bank is enough for the same Q (but the capacitor must withstand a higher voltage).
          Design steps: ① tan θ1 = 0.75, tan θ2 = 0.3287. ② QC = 480(0.75 − 0.3287) kVAR. ③ CΔ = QC/(3·ω·600²). ④ Tap the knob and type in the value you found.
          Do not reconnect a capacitor bank right after disconnecting it: if a charged capacitor is connected in opposite phase, a very large inrush current flows. Banks are equipped with discharge resistors and contactor interlocks.
